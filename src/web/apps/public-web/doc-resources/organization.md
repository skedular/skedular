# Organizations

## Overview

Organizations are the foundation of every Skedular product.

An organization represents the business, team, workspace, venue, or individual that owns and manages bookable resources. Almost everything in Skedular belongs to an organization, making it the central place where resources, users, settings, billing, and analytics are managed.

If you want to share or rent out resources, you must first create an organization.

If you simply want to book resources owned by someone else, you do not need to create an organization.

---

# Why Organizations Exist

Organizations provide a secure boundary around everything you own and manage.

They allow you to:

- Own locations
- Manage resources
- Create teams
- Invite members
- Configure organization settings
- Manage billing and subscriptions
- View analytics
- Define booking rules
- Configure permissions
- Manage tags and zones

Every resource in Skedular belongs to exactly one organization.

---

# Organization Types

Skedular supports multiple types of organizations depending on the product being used.

## Private Organizations

Private organizations are used by **Skedular Teams**.

These organizations manage internal workplaces where employees or members book company resources such as desks, meeting rooms, parking spaces, and equipment.

Resources are generally available only to members of the organization.

Examples include:

- Businesses
- Government agencies
- Universities
- Schools
- Hospitals
- Internal departments

---

## Marketplace Organizations

Marketplace organizations are used by **Skedular Spaces**.

These organizations operate shared workspaces, coworking spaces, serviced offices, studios, meeting venues, or any business that rents resources to external customers.

Marketplace organizations can make their resources available publicly or privately.

Examples include:

- Coworking spaces
- Meeting room providers
- Shared offices
- Event venues
- Training centres

---

## Host Organizations

Host organizations are used by **Skedular Host**.

They are designed for individuals or small businesses who simply want to rent out one or more places without the complexity of managing a full coworking operation.

The experience is intentionally simplified while still providing the same powerful booking engine.

Examples include:

- Home offices
- Studios
- Small meeting spaces
- Private offices
- Event spaces
- Storage facilities

---

# What Belongs to an Organization?

An organization owns and manages all of its operational data.

This includes:

- Locations
- Resources
- Teams
- Members
- Tags
- Zones
- Bookings
- Products (Spaces and Host)
- Memberships
- Subscriptions
- Customers
- Payments
- Analytics
- Organization settings

Every one of these entities is scoped to a single organization.

---

# Members and Permissions

Organizations can have multiple members.

Each member is assigned one or more roles that determine what they are allowed to do.

Typical roles include:

- Owner
- Administrator
- Member

Depending on their permissions, members may be able to:

- Manage locations
- Create resources
- Manage bookings
- Invite users
- Configure organization settings
- Access analytics
- Manage billing

Permissions help ensure that users only have access to the features they need.

---

# Locations

Organizations can contain one or many locations.

Each location represents a physical place where resources are available.

Examples include:

- Offices
- Buildings
- Floors
- Coworking spaces
- Event venues
- Warehouses

Locations help organise resources into manageable physical spaces.

---

# Resources

Resources are always owned by an organization.

Examples include:

- Desks
- Meeting rooms
- Parking spaces
- Equipment
- Studios
- Offices
- Event spaces

Without an organization, resources cannot exist.

---

# Teams

Organizations may create one or more teams.

Teams help organise members into logical groups and can be used for:

- Booking permissions
- Visibility
- Reporting
- Collaboration

Private organizations commonly make extensive use of teams.

---

# Tags and Zones

Organizations define their own tags and zones.

These are shared across all locations and resources within the organization.

This ensures consistent classification and searching throughout the workspace.

---

# Billing and Subscriptions

All commercial aspects of Skedular are managed at the organization level.

This includes:

- Subscription plans
- Billing
- Payments
- Invoices
- Usage limits
- Plan upgrades
- Payment methods

Organizations control how their services are billed and managed.

---

# Analytics

Analytics are generated for each organization.

Organization administrators can monitor information such as:

- Resource utilisation
- Booking activity
- Team usage
- Location performance
- Occupancy trends
- Customer activity
- Revenue (Spaces and Host)

Analytics help organizations understand how their resources are being used and support better operational decisions.

---

# Booking Rules

Organizations define the rules that control how their resources are booked.

Examples include:

- Availability
- Opening hours
- Booking windows
- Cancellation policies
- Membership requirements
- Resource permissions
- Access restrictions

These rules apply across the organization's resources.

---

# Creating an Organization

When creating an organization, administrators typically:

1. Create the organization.
2. Configure organization settings.
3. Add locations.
4. Create resources.
5. Configure tags and zones.
6. Invite members.
7. Configure booking rules.
8. Start accepting bookings.

For marketplace and host organizations, administrators may also configure products, pricing, payments, and public listings.

---

# Things to Know

- Every resource belongs to exactly one organization.
- Organizations can contain multiple locations.
- Organizations can contain multiple teams.
- Organizations manage their own members and permissions.
- Tags and zones are defined at the organization level.
- Billing and subscriptions are managed per organization.
- Analytics are generated per organization.
- Booking rules are configured at the organization level.
- Users can belong to multiple organizations.
- You only need an organization if you want to manage or share resources. Users booking resources owned by others do not need to create one.

---

# Best Practices

For the best experience:

- Create separate organizations for independent businesses.
- Use locations to represent physical places.
- Keep tags and zones consistent across the organization.
- Assign appropriate member roles and permissions.
- Regularly review analytics to optimise resource utilisation.
- Keep organization settings and billing information up to date.
